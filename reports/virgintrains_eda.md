## 6. Data quality issues

- **Unreliable `inbound` flag**: 8 accounts flagged `inbound=True` behave like other operators' agents (caret sign-offs). Treated as `other_agent`; raw flag preserved.
- **Missing relationship ids**: `in_response_to_tweet_id` is empty for 39.4% of customer tweets (expected: thread openers) but also for 1.1% of VirginTrains agent tweets (a real gap: replies whose parent is not recorded; `response_tweet_id` is empty for 31.4% of all tweets). Parent ids that point at tweets absent from the file: 91 tweets. Every VirginTrains conversation has >= 2 tweets (the dataset contains no single-tweet VirginTrains threads), so the @mention orphan-linking fallback attached 0 tweets; it matters only for other brands.
- **Revived threads**: 361 threads contain a silence longer than 24 h; 142 contain a gap longer than a week. 361 tweets pre-date 2017-10-09 (back to 2012), i.e. people replying into old threads.
- **Multi-customer threads**: 1,231 threads contain several customers; they are split into one case per customer, but replies from a customer to another customer remain visible context only in the thread table.
- **Unattributed tweets**: 246 agent tweets that answer nobody in the data (announcements, replies to tweets outside the sample) are excluded from cases; their ids are kept in the tweets table with `exclusion_reason`.
- **Cases without a brand reply**: 8.1% (often customers replying to other customers under an announcement). 9 cases have no customer message at all.
- **Public view only**: 4.0% of cases show a DM request; anything resolved after that is not observable, so `resolved` and `redirected_to_dm` under-describe real handling.
- **Heuristic labels**: resolution types come from regexes (e.g. `refund` = refund discussed, including denials; `compensation` includes any 'claim' wording). Precision has been spot-checked, not measured.
- **Weak cluster structure**: silhouette <= 0.05 and low agreement with a TF-IDF clustering (see section 4).
- **Templated brand replies**: a few canned replies ('we are awaiting an update as to when ...') account for much of the 'information_provided' mass, so reply-grounding metrics may reward template matching.
- **Encoding**: stored text is untouched; only a handful of tweets contain genuinely corrupted characters (mojibake).
- **Time window**: almost all tweets fall in a seven-week window around Oct-Dec 2017, so event-driven topics (a specific disruption week) are over-represented and temporal generalisation cannot be assessed.

**Why merge status/delay clusters:** 1/2: 1 shared of top templates; 1/9: 4 shared of top templates; 2/9: 2 shared of top templates. Their resolution mix is the same (mostly `information_provided`, then `self_service` links to Live Updates).

**What to do about the weak spots before building on it:**

- `chitchat_non_support` is **not** clean chit-chat: agents answered with substantive information in 39% of its cases, and its low-margin examples include real complaints. Treat it as 'low-signal opener', not as 'safe to ignore'.
- `journey_disruption_complaint` is a catch-all with no clear question; expect noisy labels. Hand-label both catch-all clusters first in the golden set.
- `customer_service_complaint` and `delay_repay_refund_claim` have the highest DM-redirect rates; these are the best candidates for human escalation, but only a human reviewer can confirm that.
- No cluster separates lost property, accessibility assistance, or safety incidents; they are rare and hidden inside `journey_disruption_complaint`. Consider adding them as explicit escalation triggers rather than intents.
- `unclear_or_media_only` is a necessary fallback intent: some openers contain only a mention, photo, or link.
- `safe_to_auto_handle` is `NEEDS_REVIEW` for every intent. The hints above only say where historic agent behaviour is templated or low-risk, not that automation is safe.
